What each tool sees, and what we fix.

Meta, Triple Whale, Shopify and your own dashboard each count sales from Meta ads differently, so they give different numbers. We compared all four for the same dates. This page explains why they differ, what we found in the tracking setup, and which number to use for which decision.

Summary

What we confirmed

Shopify, Triple Whale and your dashboard agree with each other on Meta's sales, within 1 to 3 orders. Meta's own report is the one that's higher.

Why Meta is higher

Meta also counts people who only saw an ad and never clicked. It also follows people across phones and laptops, which store tracking can't do.

What we'll fix

Add Triple Whale's tracking tags to the Meta ads, and switch Meta to count clicks only (with your OK). We also list a few setup suggestions for your team.

Why the numbers differ

Why Meta reports more sales

In short Two reasons explain most of the gap. One thing we checked turned out not to be a cause.

1
Biggest reason

Meta counts people who only saw an ad

10 of Meta's 22 sales ($879) came from people who saw an ad but never clicked it.

  • Meta's default setting is "7-day click + 1-day view". A sale counts if the buyer clicked an ad in the past 7 days, or only saw one in the past day.
  • Each sale is counted once: 12 sales from clicks plus 10 from views makes 22.
  • Most of your sales come from YouTube, email and Google. Some of those buyers also saw a Meta ad the day before, and Meta counts the sale too.
  • Store-side tools only see clicks, so they never count these sales.
2
Different rules

Meta and the store tools count clicks differently

Even counting clicks only, Meta finds 12 sales. The store-side tools find 3 to 6.

  • Meta counts any click from the past 7 days. Because people are logged in to Facebook or Instagram, it can follow them from phone to laptop.
  • Store-side tools give the sale to the last link clicked in the same browser. If someone clicks a Meta ad and later comes back through an email or Google, that channel gets the sale.
  • Instagram's built-in browser: tapping an ad opens a browser inside the Instagram app. If the person buys later in Safari or on a laptop, store-side tools can't link the two.
3
Checked, not a cause

The Meta pixel fires twice, but Meta still counts once

Both copies carry the same ID, so Meta merges them. Sales are not counted twice. Item D below shows the screenshot.

Setup check

What we found in the tracking setup

In short One thing we'll fix, and four things for your team to check or consider.

A

The Meta ads are missing Triple Whale's tracking tags

In short: Triple Whale can't fully read our Meta links, so it may miss some Meta sales and can't tell which ad sold.

Every Meta ad link carries tags (UTM tags) that tell tracking tools where the visitor came from. Triple Whale needs two specific ones: a source tag (tw_source) and an ad ID tag (tw_adid). Our links have tags for your dashboard, but not these two.

utm_source=meta&utm_medium=paid&utm_campaign={{campaign.id}}&utm_content={{ad.id}}&tw_source={{site_source_name}}&tw_adid={{ad.id}}

The green part gets added. Your dashboard keeps reading the rest, exactly as today.

We fix
B

Most orders in Triple Whale have no channel

In short: Triple Whale can't tell where 62% of buyers came from, because many links aren't tagged.

In the last 14 days, 93 of 151 orders show as "Direct" or "Non-attributed". Links without tags (emails, YouTube descriptions, affiliate links) end up there. Adding tags would show which channel brought each buyer. This is a suggestion; your team knows best which links can be tagged.

Suggestion
C

Meta data sharing in Shopify: confirm it's on Maximum

In short: one setting in Shopify decides how much purchase data Meta receives. It should be on Maximum.

The setting is in Meta's Facebook & Instagram app inside Shopify. On Maximum, each purchase is sent to Meta from the shopper's browser and also directly from Shopify's server (called the Conversions API). Meta merges the two. That gives Meta the most complete purchase data, even when a browser blocks tracking.

  1. In Shopify: Shopify admin → Sales channels → Facebook & Instagram → Settings → Data sharing. Customer data sharing should be on, set to Maximum (the choices are Standard, Enhanced and Maximum).
  2. In Meta: Events Manager → Data sources → pixel 259583715157950 → Overview → Purchase. It should show events from both the browser and the server, and an Event Match Quality score of Good or better. This score shows how well Meta can match purchases to people.

We already see server purchase events in Meta, so it's probably already on Maximum. This is a quick confirmation.

Confirm
D

The Meta pixel fires twice, but Meta counts it once

In short: a duplicate, but harmless for the numbers. Cleaning it up is optional.

The screenshot is from Meta Pixel Helper, a Chrome extension that shows what the Meta pixel sends. Each page sends PageView and ViewContent twice: once from the Facebook & Instagram app, and once from a custom pixel in Shopify's Customer Events (the rows with web-pixels@…). Both copies carry the same event ID (for example sh-142999ba-AFD6-48EB-A38F-4BE639AC0998). Meta treats events with the same ID as one, so nothing is counted twice.

Not a counting issue
E

Google Analytics may be counting visits two or three times

In short: Google Analytics is set up in three places. If all three are active, its visit and purchase numbers are inflated.

The same Google Analytics property (G-V6B5W05B3L) is set up in Google Tag Manager, in the Google & YouTube app, and in a custom pixel in Shopify. If all three send data, every visit and purchase can be counted more than once, so Google Analytics reports read too high. This doesn't affect Google Ads, which only uses the Google & YouTube app.

How to check it
  1. Chrome DevTools: open a product page, press F12, open the Network tab and type collect?v=2 in the filter box. Reload the page. Each request that contains G-V6B5W05B3L and page_view is one counted visit. One is right; two or three means duplicates.
  2. Google Tag Assistant (tagassistant.google.com): connect videoeditorstudio.com and open the G-V6B5W05B3L tag. If a page shows page_view more than once, it's duplicated.
  3. Compare with Shopify: for the same few days, compare purchases in Google Analytics (Reports → Monetization) with Shopify's order count. About 2x or 3x more in Google Analytics confirms it.
How to fix it

Keep Google Analytics in one place only. We suggest the Google & YouTube app: it's Shopify's official connection, it tracks checkout purchases, and Google Ads already relies on it.

  1. Google Tag Manager (container GTM-54QPPKXD): pause the Google Analytics tags that use G-V6B5W05B3L, then publish. Leave other tags alone, including the Google Ads tag.
  2. Shopify: Shopify admin → Settings → Customer events. Open the custom pixel that has Google Analytics code in it (look for gtag or G-V6B5W05B3L). Remove that code, or disconnect the pixel if that's all it does. Don't touch the custom pixel your dashboard uses.
  3. Check again with DevTools: one page_view per page. Over the next few days, Google Analytics purchases should match Shopify orders.

Not only tracking

Meta's results are weak as well

Better tracking will make the numbers more exact, but it won't lift Meta to 1.3x on its own.

  • Last 14 days in Triple Whale: $677 spent, 3 sales, $187 back (0.28x). Reaching 1.3x at this spend needs about 3 times as many sales.
  • People who come from Meta rarely buy: about 1 order per 1,000 visits, vs 9 for Google ads and 41 for email (your dashboard, last 28 days).
  • Orders are small, because the product carousel in the ads leads buyers to $19–59 packs.
  • The whole store slowed last week: 50 orders, vs 101 the week before.

What these terms mean
ROAS
Revenue divided by ad spend. 0.5x means every $1 spent brought back $0.50.
Attribution
Deciding which channel gets the credit for a sale.
Last click
The sale goes to the last link the buyer clicked before ordering.
First click
The sale goes to the first link that brought the buyer to the store.
7-day click + 1-day view
Meta's default. It counts a sale if the buyer clicked an ad in the past 7 days, or saw one in the past day.
7-day click
Meta counts a sale only if the buyer clicked an ad in the past 7 days.
Store-side tracking
Tools on your store (Triple Whale, Shopify, your dashboard) that see the clicks people make before buying.
Tracking tags (UTM)
Labels added to a link so tracking tools know where the visitor came from.
Direct / Non-attributed
Orders where no tagged link was found, so no channel gets the credit.
Meta pixel
A small piece of code on the store that tells Meta what visitors do, such as viewing a product or buying.
Event ID
A label on each thing the pixel sends. When two have the same name and ID, Meta counts them once.
Conversions API
Sending purchases to Meta directly from Shopify's server, not only from the shopper's browser.
Event Match Quality
Meta's score for how well it can match purchases to real people. Higher is better.
