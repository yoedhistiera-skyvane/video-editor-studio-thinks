What these terms mean
CPA
Cost per purchase. Ad spend divided by the number of purchases.
AOV
Average order value. How much a buyer pays per order, on average.
ROAS
Return on ad spend. Sales divided by ad spend. 6.26x means every $1 spent brought back $6.26 in sales.
CPM
Cost per 1,000 views. How much it costs to show the ads 1,000 times.
Reach
How many different people saw the ads.
Frequency
How many times each person saw the ads, on average.
CPC
Cost per click on the ad link.
Lookalike
People Meta finds who are similar to past buyers.
Remarketing
Showing ads again to people who visited the store and added to cart or started checkout, but didn't buy.
Advantage+ audience
A Meta setting that lets it show ads beyond the interests we chose, if it finds likely buyers.
ABO
Budget set for each ad set separately, so each product gets a fair, equal share.
UTM tag
A label added to the ad link so Shopify can tell which campaign and ad a sale came from.

Why it happened: what the data shows

1. Meta moved the budget to this ad after the other ads started selling

The other 3 ads made Core's sales on Sep 12 and 13. From Sep 13 to 16, Meta spent $17.83 on Content Creator A and only $3.71 on those 3 ads together. Meta chooses automatically which ad in the group gets the money, and it kept choosing this one even though it had no sales.

3. Most of the money went to the Facebook feed

About 60% of its spend ($13.22) ran in the Facebook feed. That brought 16 page views and 0 add-to-carts. On the Instagram feed each click cost $1.13, the most of any placement.

4. Possible reason: the ad talks to a different buyer than Core Audience targets

Core Audience targets video editing software, cinematography, film editing, video production and DaVinci interests, plus people who list video editor or videographer as their job. The Content Creator ad says "Built for YouTube and social content creators", and the website lists this bundle under "Social & YouTube". Transition Bundle is listed under "Any editor". So the ad may be reaching editors and filmmakers who aren't the buyer this pack is made for. This fits the numbers, but the data can't prove it.

Did it bring in people who then bought a different pack?

Nothing in the data shows that:

  • If someone clicked this ad and bought any pack within 7 days, Meta would credit the sale to this ad, unless they clicked a different ad before buying. It shows 0 purchases, so that didn't happen within what Meta tracks.
  • A sale would go to another ad only if the person clicked that other ad before buying. 10 people had clicked Content Creator A by the end of Sep 13, when Core's 3 sales happened, so it's possible but not something Meta can show. Checking those orders in Shopify can confirm it.
What this meansFewer people click this ad, and the biggest drop comes after the click, on the product page. Pausing this ad lets the budget go back to the ads that sell. If Content Creator Bundle is tested again, give it its own ad set and aim it at YouTube and social creators, as in the ABO product test.

Daily, placement and age data were pulled later on Sep 17 and include part of that day, so they don't add up exactly to the totals above.

F6
Tracking

Core's ads have the campaign name typed into their UTM tags

Fixed by S6

One typo or an old campaign name here would send sales to the wrong campaign in Shopify.

A name typed by hand is easy to get wrong. If it isn't updated every time a campaign is created or renamed, Shopify reports sales under the wrong campaign.
